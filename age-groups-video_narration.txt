One number can’t tell every story.

In the United States, the National Cancer Institute estimated that fourteen thousand nine hundred ten people ages zero to nineteen would be diagnosed with cancer in twenty twenty-four.

That estimate includes nine thousand six hundred twenty children ages zero to fourteen, and five thousand two hundred ninety adolescents ages fifteen to nineteen.

These are estimates for populations, not predictions for an individual child. Childhood and adolescent cancers are not one disease, and care needs differ.

Bring questions about the diagnosis, tests, treatment options, and follow-up to the pediatric oncology team.

Source: National Cancer Institute, Cancer in Children and Adolescents, updated August twenty-seventh, twenty twenty-four. General education only, not medical advice.
