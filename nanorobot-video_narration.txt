Could tiny robots help guide medicine toward childhood tumors? Researchers are exploring micro and nanorobots, but this is emerging science, not an established treatment.

A recent narrative review describes a neuroblastoma microrobot tested in vitro, and a retinoblastoma catheter system tested in benchtop vascular phantoms.

Those are promising engineering steps, not proof of benefit in children. The review did not identify an interventional pediatric cancer trial of the robotic platforms it examined.

Before clinical use, researchers must show that actuation adds benefit, navigation can be tracked, materials clear safely, and manufacturing is reliable.

The evidence today is preclinical. Families should discuss treatment with a pediatric oncology team.

Source: Micro- and Nanorobots for Targeted Drug Delivery in Childhood Cancer, a literature-based narrative review, October 2026. Educational information, not medical advice.
